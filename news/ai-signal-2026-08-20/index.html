AI Signals: Markets, Labor, Education, and Regulation in Tension

Tech giants face doubts about the profitability of their AI bets, while employment polarizes. Regulation and education emerge as key to balanced development.

20 August 20264 min read

Artificial intelligence is advancing at a pace that overflows traditional frameworks of investment, work, and policy. Today, news converges showing the magnitude of AI spending, uncertainty about its return, the bifurcation of the labor market, the educational gender gap, and regulatory responses and specialized applications. This edition connects these fronts to offer a practical view of opportunities and limits.

Markets: The Spending Iceberg and the Shadow of Profitability

Tech giants have bet their future on AI, but markets are beginning to worry about the sustainability of these investments. An analysis by Bitfinanzas reveals that real spending could be up to $3 trillion higher than reported, due to hidden costs in infrastructure, research, and acquisitions. La Vanguardia points out that doubts about accounts and profitability are already generating volatility in the sector. For investors, the key is to distinguish between narrative and fundamentals: not every AI investment will translate into short-term profits.

Work: Jevons Paradox vs. Bifurcated Talent Economy

The labor impact of AI is debated. Pictet Asset Management argues that, due to the Jevons paradox, efficiency can generate more demand and net employment. However, Expansión describes a talent economy split in two: high-skilled jobs with growing demand and low-skilled jobs easily automatable. Both views are not exclusive: growth can create new occupations, but it requires rapid adaptation of skills. For professionals, the priority is to invest in technical and digital skills that place them in the higher-value segment.

Education: Personalization with AI and the Persistent Gap

AI offers tools to personalize learning, such as the innovation project that analyzes data to design educational programs in Teruel. But a study by EL PAÍS warns that adolescent girls start with a disadvantage in learning this technology due to stereotypes and lack of opportunities. If not intervened, the gender gap will widen in the tech sector. Educational institutions and edtech companies must integrate AI with an inclusive approach, fostering female participation from an early age.

Regulation and Public Sector: From Emotional Chatbots to Housing

China has decided to limit romantic relationships between users and artificial intelligence, an example of how public policies shape the affective development of these technologies. In Catalonia, the government will use AI to locate empty apartments and bring them to market, analyzing water and energy consumption data. These initiatives show AI's potential to solve social problems, but also the need to establish ethical and legal boundaries. For companies seeking public contracts, transparency and respect for privacy will be decisive.

Legal Tech: Reliability and Open Training

The legal sector is advancing in specific solutions. An AI created for the legal field has managed to eliminate hallucinations, improving research accuracy. Additionally, Harvey launches a free and open certification for legal engineers, combining programming and law. These tools reduce errors and costs, but require professionals capable of integrating them. For lawyers and technicians, obtaining this certification can be a competitive advantage in a market that increasingly values the intersection between technology and law.

What matters

  1. For investors: evaluate real AI spending beyond what's reported and consider uncertainty about short-term profitability.
  2. For workers: develop technical and digital skills to access the high-skill segment and avoid automation.
  3. For educators and edtech companies: use AI to personalize learning, but with programs that reduce the gender gap in technology.
  4. For the public sector: AI can help with housing and other services, but requires regulatory frameworks that protect citizens.
  5. For legal professionals: take advantage of free certifications in legal AI and tools that eliminate hallucinations to increase efficiency.

Artificial intelligence is not a monolithic force: its effects depend on investment decisions, educational policies, regulatory frameworks, and sectoral adoption. The tensions between optimism and skepticism, between efficiency and inequality, demand a critical and pragmatic view. Only then can its transformative potential be harnessed without leaving anyone behind.
