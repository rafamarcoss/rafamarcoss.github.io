AI: Hidden Spending, Divided Talent, and the Education Gap

Big tech spends 3 trillion more than it appears, while employment splits in two. China regulates emotional bonds with chatbots, and education shows gender gaps.

19 August 20264 min read

From financial markets to classrooms, artificial intelligence is generating both opportunities and tensions. In this edition, we connect the dots between tech's hidden spending, employment polarization, legal innovations, and educational gaps.

Markets: The Spending Iceberg and Private Bets

Bitfinanzas reveals that big tech's real spending on AI is $3 trillion higher than it appears, due to hidden costs that don't show up on traditional balance sheets. Meanwhile, Finanzas.com explains how an investment in Anthropic could quadruple before its IPO, highlighting the return potential in private AI startups. This tension between massive capital outlays and potential outsized gains forces investors to scrutinize every opportunity, as not all bets will be profitable.

Work: The New Talent Economy Splits Employment in Two

Expansión describes how AI is splitting employment into two economies: one with high demand for tech talent and another with more routine jobs vulnerable to automation. Seguritecnia offers a practical guide to identifying which jobs are safe from AI, a useful resource for professionals wanting to anticipate changes in their sector. Additionally, Negocios.com lists six common mistakes in corporate AI training that hinder productivity; correcting them is key to preventing organizations from widening the talent gap.

Education: Tools Within Reach, but with a Gender Gap

Unite.AI presents a list of the top 10 AI tools for education in August 2026, with options to personalize learning and automate teaching tasks. However, EL PAÍS warns that teenage girls start at a disadvantage in learning AI due to social and educational factors, requiring specific policies to close the gender gap. The availability of technology alone does not guarantee equitable adoption; a deliberate effort is needed in classrooms and in the design of inclusive programs.

Regulation and Trust: From China to the Courts

China is taking steps to prevent its internet users from falling in love with artificial intelligences, a sign of concern over the emotional impact of these systems. In the legal sphere, El Español reports on an AI designed for the sector that eliminates hallucinations in courts, while Dentons addresses how AI is transforming mergers and acquisitions, from legal due diligence to creating a 'Business Value Playbook'. These advances reflect that reliable AI adoption requires both technical precision and regulatory frameworks that evolve at the same pace.

What matters

  1. Companies should review their AI training programs to avoid the six mistakes that hinder productivity, according to Negocios.com.
  2. Investors should consider both the hidden costs of AI spending and the potential returns in private startups, but evaluate each case rigorously.
  3. Professionals should assess the vulnerability of their jobs and develop skills aligned with the talent economy described by Expansión.
  4. Educators can leverage AI tools, but must actively address the gender gap detected in teenage girls' learning.
  5. Regulation and technical precision are key to reliable AI adoption in sectors like law, as shown by the cases of China and new legal tools.

Artificial intelligence is transforming every field, but success will depend on how we manage the tensions between investment, skills, and trust. Today's news shows both promises and risks; acting with informed strategy is the only way to turn disruption into opportunity.
