AI: Opportunities and Risks in Markets, Employment, and Education

AI drives public policies and record valuations, but also opens labor and educational gaps. Markets doubt the profitability of big tech and cybersecurity faces new threats.

21 August 20264 min read

The day of August 21, 2026 leaves a complex landscape: artificial intelligence consolidates as an engine of innovation, but tensions arise in its adoption. From Catalonia, which will use AI to locate empty homes, to the valuation of Anthropic, which multiplies fourfold before its IPO, technology shows its potential. However, doubts about the profitability of big tech, the division of the labor market, and the negative effects on learning reveal that not everything is promises. This article connects the threads of markets, work, education, regulation, and cybersecurity to understand where we are and where we are going.

Markets: Euphoria and Doubts About Profitability

Big tech has bet its future on AI, but markets are beginning to worry about their accounts and profitability, according to La Vanguardia. Meanwhile, Finanzas.com reports that an investment in Anthropic has multiplied fourfold before its IPO, reflecting the appeal of AI startups. This dichotomy between very high valuations and uncertain profits generates volatility. For investors, the lesson is clear: the potential return is high, but so is the risk. Diversifying and analyzing fundamentals becomes essential.

Employment and Training: A Widening Gap

Expansión describes a labor market split in two: AI creates a new talent economy but leaves behind those who do not adapt. Negocios.com adds that companies make six common mistakes when training in AI, which hinders productivity. The combination of both factors threatens to widen inequality. For professionals, continuous updating is key; for companies, avoiding these mistakes (such as not aligning training with objectives or not measuring results) can make the difference between leveraging AI or falling behind.

Education: Gender Gap and Declining Learning

El País warns that adolescent girls start at a disadvantage in learning AI, which perpetuates the lack of diversity in the sector. On the other hand, La Vanguardia publishes a study showing that using AI to do homework reduces learning by 20%. These data underscore the need for a balanced approach: AI can be an educational tool, but its excessive use limits skill development. Parents and educators must encourage critical use and close the gender gap from an early age.

Regulation and Specific Sectors: Advances in Legal and Housing

The legal sector is taking steps toward reliability: El Español presents an AI created to eliminate hallucinations in courts, and Esade analyzes the impact and tools available for lawyers in Spain. These developments show how AI can be successfully integrated into regulated environments. In the public sphere, Catalonia will use AI to locate empty homes and bring them to market, according to El Periódico. Both news reflect a pattern: AI applied to specific problems, with supervision and clear objectives, can generate tangible benefits.

Cybersecurity: The New Digital Offensive

Computerworld España warns that AI is creating digital offensives that surpass traditional defenses. This represents a paradigm shift: attacks become more sophisticated and automated. Organizations must adopt advanced security measures, also based on AI, to detect and respond in real time. Cybersecurity is no longer an option but a critical necessity in the AI era.

What matters

  1. AI offers high investment returns but with high volatility; diversify and evaluate fundamentals.
  2. AI training is key to avoid widening the labor gap; avoid common training mistakes.
  3. Excessive use of AI in education reduces learning; foster critical thinking and address the gender gap.
  4. Regulated sectors advance toward reliable AI, but cybersecurity requires constant updating.
  5. Public policies can leverage AI for social problems, such as housing, always with supervision.

Artificial intelligence is a powerful tool that is already transforming our economy and society. However, today's news shows that its adoption is not without risks: from market uncertainty to social gaps and security threats. The challenge is to harness its potential without ignoring its limits, with inclusive educational policies, responsible investments, and regulatory frameworks that ensure ethical use. Only then can we make AI a driver of shared progress.
