AI Splits Markets, Work, and Education: Opportunities and Limits

Artificial intelligence is redefining the talent economy and the legal sector, while investors doubt short-term profitability. At the same time, its use in education reveals risks to learning and equality.

22 August 20264 min read

The integration of artificial intelligence is advancing on multiple fronts, creating a landscape of opportunities contrasted with significant limits. While big tech faces market skepticism over profitability, figures like Warren Buffett maintain confidence in companies like Alphabet. In the workplace and legal sphere, AI promises efficiency but requires proper training that many companies have yet to master. Meanwhile, education faces the challenge of the tool reducing academic performance and worsening gender gaps, all while public administration exploits its utility to solve social problems like housing.

Markets and Investment: Between Skepticism and Confidence

Big tech has bet its future on AI, but markets are beginning to worry about doubts over their accounts and profitability. However, this market caution contrasts with the view of benchmark investors: Warren Buffett has invested in Alphabet, indicating underlying confidence in the long-term value of AI-driven companies. Furthermore, the market is evolving from simple virtual assistants toward a boom in AI agents, opening new business and automation opportunities.

Work and the Legal Sector: The New Talent Economy

The impact on employment is splitting the market in two, creating a new talent economy that HR professionals must manage strategically. In this context, training is key, but companies are making six common mistakes when training their employees in AI, which hampers productivity. The legal sector is a clear example of transformation: AI is changing the business of law firms. To this end, a specific AI has been created that eliminates hallucinations in courtrooms, improving reliability and solving a critical problem in the sector.

Education and Society: Limits in Learning and Gender Gaps

Technological adoption in classrooms presents evident tensions. Using AI to do homework and assignments reduces student learning by 20%, a crucial fact for educators and edtech companies. This limitation is compounded by a gender gap: teenage girls start at a disadvantage in learning AI, requiring policies to correct this inequality. In contrast to these educational challenges, public administration demonstrates practical utility; Catalonia will use AI to locate empty apartments and bring them to market, showing how technology can serve the public interest.

What matters

  1. Investors must weigh market caution on tech profitability against long-term bets like Buffett's on Alphabet.
  2. Companies should review their AI training programs to avoid the six common mistakes that hamper employee productivity.
  3. Law firms can optimize their business by adopting specialized AI that eliminates hallucinations in judicial settings.
  4. Educators should limit AI use in school assignments to avoid the 20% drop in academic performance.
  5. Public administrations can leverage AI to manage structural problems, such as locating vacant housing.

Artificial intelligence is no longer a future promise but a reality that segments employment, transforms established sectors, and redraws learning. For professionals, investors, and students, success will depend on balancing the adoption of its operational advantages with rigorous management of its educational and market risks. The key is to apply technology judiciously, ensuring that efficiency does not compromise talent development or information reliability.
